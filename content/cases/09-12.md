title: "Case 9 · Payments gateway (Razorpay/Stripe-style): transaction reliability"
category: "Fintech|Platform|Reliability"
order: 9
summary: Raise payment success rates for merchants by attacking failure causes — and make reliability a product.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    Razorpay and Stripe are payment platforms used by businesses to accept online payments. This case is a **hypothetical** payments-platform scenario; numbers are invented and say nothing about either company.

**Business background.** A payments platform earns per successful transaction; merchants choose providers largely on success rate, cost and developer experience.

**Target users.** Merchants (developers, finance teams) and their end customers paying online.

**Customer problem.** Merchants see unexplained payment failures at peak (sale days), lose revenue, and can't tell whether the cause is the bank, the customer or the gateway.

**Business objective.** Increase overall payment success rate and merchant retention; reduce support tickets about failures.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).**

```chart
type: pareto
title: Failure causes (hypothetical, 10,000 failed payments)
items: [["Bank / issuer timeout", 3100], ["Customer abandoned 3-D Secure / OTP", 2400], ["Insufficient funds", 1700], ["Incorrect card / UPI details", 1300], ["Gateway errors", 900], ["Fraud blocks", 600]]
caption: Hypothetical. Some causes are addressable by product (retries, routing, UX), others are not (insufficient funds).
```

**Assumptions.** Smart routing to healthier bank/acquirer paths reduces timeouts; better authentication UX reduces abandonment; real-time failure reasons reduce merchant tickets.

**Product hypothesis.** *Health-based routing plus a merchant-facing failure dashboard will raise success rate by 1.5 points and cut failure-related tickets by 25%.*

**User journey (merchant).** Integrate API → go live → monitor dashboard → investigate failures → contact support → reconcile settlements.

**Candidate solutions.** A) Dynamic routing based on real-time issuer health; B) Retry with idempotency for transient errors; C) Authentication UX improvements (clear OTP screens, fallback methods); D) Merchant failure analytics with reason codes; E) Status page and proactive incident alerts.

**Prioritisation.** Cost of delay is high before the festive sale season → **A + E** first (WSJF), then D, then C.

**MVP.** Routing for the top 5 issuers by volume; incident alerts via webhook/email; reason-coded dashboard (beta) for 50 merchants.

**KPIs.** Primary: payment success rate (by method, issuer). Secondary: p95 authorisation latency; failure tickets per 1,000 transactions; merchant NPS/CSAT. Guardrails: duplicate charges (target 0), fraud rate, cost per transaction.

**Risks.** Routing changes increase cost; incorrect health signals; duplicate charges on retries (needs idempotency); regulatory requirements on authentication.

**Experiment plan.** Merchant-level or transaction-level randomisation with careful monitoring; staged rollout; incident review.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Success rate 92.0% → 93.4%; issuer timeouts −28%; tickets −22%; duplicate charges 0.

**Recommendation.** Expand routing to more issuers; make the failure dashboard GA with clear reason codes and recommended actions.

**Key PM lessons.** Reliability *is* the product for platforms; decompose failures and work on the controllable ones; developer- and finance-facing transparency reduces support load.

id: duolingo-gamification
title: "Case 10 · Duolingo-style learning app: gamification and retention"
category: "Consumer|Retention|Engagement"
order: 10
summary: Use streaks and goals to build a learning habit — without making users feel manipulated.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    Duolingo is widely known for gamification such as streaks and leaderboards (leagues). This case is a **hypothetical** language-learning scenario; numbers are invented.

**Business background.** Freemium learning app; habit formation drives retention and premium conversion.

**Target users.** Casual learners, exam preparers, travellers.

**Customer problem.** Learners lose motivation after the first week; a broken streak can make them quit entirely.

**Business objective.** Raise day-30 retention and weekly learning minutes without increasing churn from frustration.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).** Users with a 7-day streak are 3× more likely to be active at day 30 (correlation); 40% of churned users broke a streak of ≥ 10 days in their last week; interviews: “I lost my streak, so why bother.”

**Assumptions.** Protecting progress after a lapse (streak freeze/repair) prevents quitting; adaptive daily goals reduce burnout; social features help some segments but not all.

**Product hypothesis.** *Offering one free “streak repair” after a missed day and adaptive goals will raise day-30 retention by 2 points while keeping learning minutes stable.*

```chart
type: loop
title: Habit loop for daily learning
nodes: ["Cue: daily reminder at chosen time", "Routine: 5-minute lesson", "Reward: progress, streak, XP", "Investment: streak & goals worth protecting"]
kind: R
center: "Habit loop"
caption: The loop reinforces itself, but a broken streak can flip motivation to zero. Design recovery, not just pressure.
```

**Candidate solutions.** A) Streak repair; B) Adaptive daily goal; C) Friend quests; D) Leaderboards for all; E) Learning-outcome check-ins (mini tests).

**Prioritisation.** RICE favours A and B (broad reach, low effort); D risks demotivating low performers → opt-in only.

**MVP.** A + B for new users in one market.

**KPIs.** Primary: day-30 retention. Leading: 7-day streak rate. Guardrails: lesson quality (accuracy), notification opt-outs, reported anxiety/frustration in surveys, learning outcomes (test scores).

**Risks.** Gaming metrics (very easy lessons to keep streaks); dark-pattern perception; wellbeing.

**Experiment plan.** A/B for 6 weeks; survey sub-sample about motivation and pressure.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Day-30 retention 18.0% → 20.3%; learning minutes flat; opt-outs unchanged; self-reported pressure slightly lower.

**Recommendation.** Ship streak repair; refine adaptive goals; add learning-outcome measures to the North Star.

**Key PM lessons.** Gamification must serve the user's real goal (learning); design for lapses; pair engagement metrics with outcome and wellbeing guardrails.

title: "Case 11 · Notion-style workspace: product-led growth"
category: "B2B SaaS|Growth|PLG"
order: 11
summary: Grow from individual users to paying teams through templates, collaboration and in-product upgrades.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    Notion is commonly cited as a product-led-growth example with a free plan and a large template gallery. This case is a **hypothetical** PLG workspace scenario; numbers are invented.

**Business background.** A flexible docs/wiki/database tool; individuals sign up free; revenue comes from team plans.

**Target users.** Individual knowledge workers; team leads; workspace admins.

**Customer problem.** New users face a blank page and don't know what to build; individual users rarely invite teammates, so they never hit team value.

**Business objective.** Increase free→team conversion and expansion (seats per workspace).

**Available evidence (hypothetical).** Users who start from a template activate at 48% vs 21% from blank; workspaces with ≥ 3 active members convert at 9% vs 0.8% for single-user; template gallery used by 30% of new users.

**Assumptions.** Templates shorten time-to-value; collaboration triggers (mentions, shared pages) drive invites; upgrade prompts at limits convert better than generic prompts.

**Product hypothesis.** *Role-based template onboarding plus a “share with your team” step after first page creation will raise 14-day workspaces with ≥ 3 members from 12% to 16%.*

```chart
type: loop
title: Collaboration growth loop
nodes: ["User creates a useful page", "Shares / mentions a teammate", "Teammate joins and edits", "Team relies on workspace", "Team creates more pages"]
kind: R
caption: The loop turns individual value into team adoption — the moment monetisation becomes natural.
```

**Candidate solutions.** A) Role-based template onboarding; B) Share prompt after first useful page; C) Team trial when 3rd member joins; D) Usage-limit upgrade prompts; E) Sales-assist for PQL workspaces > 20 users.

**Prioritisation.** A and B (activation and loop entry) first; C/D next; E for larger accounts.

**MVP.** A + B for new sign-ups from work email domains.

**KPIs.** Primary: % new workspaces with ≥ 3 active members by day 14. Secondary: activation, invites per user, free→team conversion at day 60. Guardrails: spammy invites (bounce/complaint rate), user satisfaction, privacy of shared pages.

**Risks.** Over-prompting invites; templates that don't fit; enterprise security concerns.

**Experiment plan.** A/B by new workspace for 4 weeks, conversion read at day 60 with holdout.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Activation 34% → 41%; ≥ 3-member workspaces 12% → 15.2%; invite complaint rate unchanged; day-60 conversion pending.

**Recommendation.** Ship; test team trial trigger next.

**Key PM lessons.** In PLG, activation and the collaboration loop are the growth engine; monetise at moments of team value; measure loop health.

id: ai-financial-assistant
title: "Case 12 · AI-powered personal financial assistant: AI product design and responsible automation"
category: "AI|Fintech|Trust & safety"
order: 12
summary: Design a consumer money assistant that explains spending and helps with bills — with strict limits on advice and actions.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    A fictional bank app adds an AI assistant. Everything here is **hypothetical**. Financial advice and automated payments are regulated in most markets; real products need legal and compliance review. Module 12 contains a related full case on an AI *customer-support* assistant.

**Business background.** A digital bank wants deeper engagement and fewer support contacts by helping customers understand and manage their money.

**Target users.** Young professionals managing first salaries; households with recurring bills.

**Customer problem.** Customers don't know where their money goes, miss bill due dates, and find statements hard to interpret.

**Business objective.** Increase monthly active usage of money-management features and reduce late payments, while avoiding unsuitable financial advice.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).** 61% of surveyed customers can't name their top three spending categories; 14% paid at least one bill late in 6 months; support receives many “what is this charge?” queries.

**Assumptions.** Natural-language answers about the customer's own transactions are valuable; bill reminders reduce late payments; users want suggestions but not autonomous payments at first.

**Product hypothesis.** *An assistant that answers questions about the customer's own transactions with citations to those transactions, and drafts bill-payment reminders and payments for explicit approval, will reduce late payments by 20% among users and cut “what is this charge?” contacts by 25%.*

```chart
type: steps
title: Autonomy plan by capability
steps:
  - ["Explain (L0–L1)", "“Where did my money go?” with transaction citations"]
  - ["Remind (L1)", "Upcoming bills; customer chooses reminders"]
  - ["Draft (L2)", "Prepares a bill payment; customer approves each one"]
  - ["Act within limits (L3, later)", "Auto-pay known bills under a set cap, with notification and undo window"]
caption: Start with explanations and reminders; earn autonomy with evidence. No investment or credit advice in scope.
```

**Candidate solutions.** A) Spending insights Q&A (read-only); B) Bill detection and reminders; C) Payment drafting with approval; D) Budget coaching; E) Investment suggestions (excluded — regulated advice).

**Prioritisation.** Value vs risk: A and B high value, low risk; C medium risk with approvals; D later; E out of scope.

**MVP.** A + B in-app for 5% of customers; English and one regional language.

**Design and controls.**
* Answers grounded in the customer's own transactions (read-only tools) and approved help content; **citations** to transactions.
* **Refuse** investment, tax and credit advice; route to human advisers or general education content.
* **Permissions** enforced in backend; no access to other customers' data.
* **No external communication** by the assistant; payments only with explicit confirmation (later phase).
* **Privacy:** minimise data in prompts; vendor terms prevent training on customer data; logs redacted.
* **Evaluation:** test set of 300 questions (category math, merchant identification, refusals, injection attempts in merchant names/notes).

**KPIs.** Primary: late-payment rate among users; Secondary: weekly active users of insights, CSAT, “what is this charge?” contacts. Quality/safety: answer accuracy (sampled), unsuitable-advice incidents (target 0), privacy incidents (target 0), refusal correctness.

**Risks and constraints.** Miscategorised transactions leading to wrong insights; perceived advice; over-reliance; prompt injection via transaction descriptions; regulatory obligations.

**Experiment plan.** Offline eval gate → 5% randomised pilot for 8 weeks → compare late payments vs control.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Category answers accurate in 94% of sampled cases (target 95% → improve merchant mapping); late payments −17% vs control; charge-query contacts −21%; 0 advice incidents; 2 injection attempts blocked in red-team.

**Recommendation.** Improve categorisation accuracy to ≥ 95%, then expand to 25%; begin design for payment drafting with approvals.

**Potential outcomes.** Best: trusted daily money companion. Base: useful insights and reminders. Worst: accuracy or advice incidents → restrict to reminders only.

**Key PM lessons.** Scope out regulated advice explicitly; ground in the customer's own data with citations; autonomy is earned; evaluation and red-teaming are launch gates.
