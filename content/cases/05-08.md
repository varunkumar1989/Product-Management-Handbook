title: "Case 5 · Spotify-style music streaming: engagement and recommendations"
category: "Consumer|Engagement|Personalisation"
order: 5
summary: Grow meaningful listening by improving discovery for new and lapsed listeners.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    Spotify is publicly known for personalised playlists such as Discover Weekly (launched 2015). This is a **hypothetical** music-streaming scenario; numbers are invented.

**Business background.** Freemium music streaming: ads on free tier, subscriptions on premium. Engagement predicts conversion and retention.

**Target users.** New free users; lapsed listeners; premium subscribers.

**Customer problem.** New users don't know what to play beyond a few favourite songs; personalisation is weak until the system learns their taste (cold start).

**Business objective.** Increase week-4 retention of new users and free→premium conversion.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).** New users who save ≥ 5 songs in week 1 retain at 46% at week 4 vs 18% for others (correlation); onboarding taste picker skipped by 52%; interviews: “the app plays the same things.”

**Assumptions.** Better cold-start recommendations increase saving behaviour; saving causes retention (to test); an onboarding taste quiz with fewer, smarter steps won't be skipped.

**Product hypothesis.** *A 3-step taste onboarding (genres → artists → mood) plus a “first mix” playlist will raise week-1 saves ≥ 5 from 28% to 35% and week-4 retention by 3 points.*

```chart
type: funnel
title: New-user engagement funnel (hypothetical cohort of 100,000)
stages: [["Installed", 100000], ["Played first song", 82000], ["Completed taste onboarding", 39000], ["Saved ≥ 5 songs in week 1", 28000], ["Active in week 4", 24500]]
caption: Hypothetical. The onboarding step loses more than half — the main lever.
```

**Candidate solutions.** A) Shorter taste onboarding; B) “First mix” playlist; C) Social import (follow friends); D) Push notifications for new releases; E) Free-tier on-demand trial.

**Prioritisation (ICE).** A 8×7×8 = 448; B 7×6×6 = 252; D 5×6×9 = 270 but risk of fatigue → guardrail; start with **A + B** together as one test.

**MVP.** New onboarding + first mix for 20% of new users in two markets.

**KPIs.** Primary: week-4 retention. Leading: week-1 saves ≥ 5. Guardrails: skip rate in first mix, notification opt-outs, streams per user quality (not just volume).

**Risks.** Taste quiz biases toward popular music; artists' fairness; measuring causality for “saves”.

**Experiment plan.** A/B for 5 weeks (to observe week 4); stratify by market.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Onboarding completion 39% → 61%; saves ≥ 5: 28% → 33%; week-4 retention 24.5% → 26.4% (+1.9 pts, CI +0.8 to +3.0); skip rate unchanged.

**Recommendation.** Ship; next test social import.

**Key PM lessons.** Fix cold start early; validate “aha” metrics causally; watch quality of engagement.

id: messaging-onboarding
title: "Case 6 · WhatsApp-style messaging: onboarding and communication workflows"
category: "Consumer|Onboarding|UX"
order: 6
summary: Get new users from install to their first meaningful conversation quickly and safely.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    WhatsApp is widely known for phone-number-based sign-up with verification codes. Your course mapped WhatsApp onboarding as a user-flow exercise (Lecture 5). This case is **hypothetical**; numbers are invented.

**Business background.** A messaging app's value depends on contacts already using it (network effects).

**Target users.** First-time smartphone users; users switching phones; small businesses.

**Customer problem.** New users drop during verification (SMS delays), contact-permission prompts, and profile setup; some never send a first message.

**Business objective.** Increase the share of installs that send a first message within 24 hours.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).**

```chart
type: funnel
title: Onboarding funnel (hypothetical, 100,000 installs)
stages: [["Install & open", 100000], ["Phone number entered", 88000], ["Code verified", 76000], ["Contacts permission granted", 58000], ["Profile done", 55000], ["First message sent in 24h", 47000]]
caption: Hypothetical. Verification (−12k) and contact permission (−18k) are the largest drops.
```

**Assumptions.** Auto-reading the verification code and voice-call fallback reduce failures; explaining *why* contacts are needed (just-in-time) increases grants; users without permission can still be productive.

**Product hypothesis.** *Verification fallback + just-in-time contact permission with a clear explanation + a “message a contact by number” path will raise first-message-in-24h from 47% to 53%.*

**User flow.** Open → enter number → receive code (SMS or call) → verify → permission ask (in context) → profile → chat list with suggestions → first message.

**Candidate solutions.** A) Code auto-fill + call fallback; B) Just-in-time contact permission with explanation; C) Skip-able profile; D) Starter suggestions (“Say hi to Priya”); E) Offline/poor-network optimisation.

**Prioritisation.** RICE: B (reach 76k, impact 2, conf 80%, effort 1) = 121,600; A (reach 88k, impact 1, conf 80%, effort 2) = 35,200; D lower confidence. **B then A.**

**MVP.** Just-in-time permission explanation + “message by number” fallback.

**KPIs.** Primary: first message within 24h. Guardrails: spam reports, permission grant rate, privacy complaints, uninstalls.

**Risks.** Privacy perceptions around contacts; spam; SMS costs; regulatory consent rules.

**Experiment plan.** A/B on new installs in 3 countries for 2 weeks.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Permission grant 76% → 82% of verified; first-message 47% → 51.5%; spam reports unchanged.

**Recommendation.** Ship B; test A next; monitor privacy feedback.

**Key PM lessons.** Ask for permissions in context with a reason; always offer a path that works without permission; network products must reach first value fast.

id: slack-channel-creation
title: "Case 7 · Slack-style collaboration: channel creation and team UX"
category: "B2B SaaS|UX|Adoption"
order: 7
summary: Make creating and finding the right channel easy so teams collaborate in the open instead of in scattered DMs.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    Slack organises work in channels. This case is a **hypothetical** team-collaboration product scenario (your project folders include earlier Slack channel-UX work you can use as a portfolio companion). Numbers are invented.

**Business background.** B2B collaboration SaaS sold per seat; value grows as teams adopt shared channels.

**Target users.** Team members, team leads, workspace admins.

**Customer problem.** New teams create duplicate or poorly named channels; people can't find the right one and fall back to DMs, which hides knowledge.

**Business objective.** Increase share of messages in channels (vs DMs) and weekly active teams per account — leading indicators of expansion.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).** 22% of channels created in the last quarter duplicate an existing topic; 35% of new channels have no purpose set; tree tests show 4 of 8 participants failed to find the “design-review” channel.

**Assumptions.** Suggesting existing channels at creation prevents duplicates; templates with purpose and naming conventions improve findability; admins want light governance.

**Product hypothesis.** *Showing “similar channels” and naming templates during creation will cut duplicate channels by 30% and raise channel-message share by 3 points.*

```chart

title: Channel creation — redesign concept
screens:
  - name: "Create channel"
    items: [["bar", "Create a channel"], ["input", "Name: proj-checkout-redesign"], ["text", "Similar channels found:"], ["row", "#proj-checkout|42 members"], ["row", "#design-reviews|18 members"], ["btn", "Join #proj-checkout instead"], ["btn", "Create new channel"]]
    notes: ["Duplicate prevention at the moment of creation.", "Naming template prefix suggests structure.", "Clear choice between joining and creating."]
  - name: "Purpose & template"
    items: [["bar", "Set up #proj-checkout-redesign"], ["input", "Purpose: Redesign checkout for Q3"], ["row", "Template|Project"], ["row", "Pinned: brief, owners, dates|auto"], ["btn", "Create"]]
    notes: ["Purpose is pre-filled from template.", "Templates add pinned essentials."]
caption: Illustrative concept based on common collaboration patterns; not a real product screenshot.
```

**Candidate solutions.** A) Similar-channel suggestions; B) Naming templates; C) Channel directory with search improvements; D) Admin archiving of inactive channels; E) AI channel summaries.

**Prioritisation.** Value vs complexity: A and B quick wins; C medium; E later (needs eval and privacy review).

**MVP.** A + B for new channels in 10% of workspaces.

**KPIs.** Primary: duplicate-channel rate; channel message share. Guardrails: channel creation volume (don't block legitimate creation), time to create, admin complaints.

**Risks.** Friction slows urgent channel creation; suggestions leak private channel names (must respect permissions).

**Experiment plan.** Workspace-level randomisation (to avoid team interference) for 4 weeks; usability test of the creation flow first (6 participants).

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Duplicates 22% → 15% (−32%); channel message share 61% → 63.5%; creation time +4 s median (acceptable).

**Recommendation.** Ship to all; add directory search next.

**Key PM lessons.** In B2B, randomise at the account/workspace level; respect permissions in suggestions; measure collaboration outcomes, not clicks.

title: "Case 8 · Card issuer (American Express-style): digital onboarding and application drop-offs"
category: "Fintech|Onboarding|Conversion"
order: 8
summary: Reduce drop-off in a digital credit-card application while meeting KYC, credit and compliance obligations.
---

!!! hypo "Hypothetical scenario"
    American Express and other issuers offer digital card applications. This case is a **hypothetical** card-issuer scenario; it does not describe any issuer's actual process, data or results. Regulatory steps are general; real processes require compliance review.

**Business background.** Digital applications for credit cards; acquisition costs are high, so each abandoned application is expensive.

**Target users.** Salaried professionals and self-employed applicants on mobile.

**Customer problem.** Long forms, document uploads that fail on mobile, unclear status after submission, and “will this affect my credit score?” anxiety.

**Business objective.** Increase submitted-application rate and approved-and-activated cards while keeping credit and fraud risk within policy.

**Available evidence (hypothetical).**

```chart
type: funnel
title: Application funnel (hypothetical month, 50,000 starts)
stages: [["Started application", 50000], ["Personal details", 41000], ["Income & employment", 33500], ["Document upload / e-KYC", 22000], ["Submitted", 20500], ["Approved", 12300], ["Activated (first use)", 9200]]
caption: Hypothetical. The largest drop is document upload / e-KYC (−11,500); approved-to-activated is also leaky.
```

Interviews (8): fear of credit-score impact; uploads failing on low light; no status after submitting.

**Assumptions.** Pre-fill and save-and-resume cut form abandonment; camera guidance improves uploads; a soft eligibility check reduces anxiety (where available and compliant); status tracking increases activation.

**Product hypothesis.** *Camera guidance + save-and-resume + clear status tracking will raise submit rate from 41% to 46% of starts with no increase in fraud or credit-loss indicators.*

**Candidate solutions.** A) Camera guidance and auto-capture; B) Save-and-resume with reminders (consented); C) Pre-fill from verified sources (with consent); D) Soft eligibility check messaging; E) Application status tracker; F) Activation nudges after approval.

**Prioritisation (weighted, hypothetical):** A 4.2, B 3.9, E 3.6, F 3.4, C 3.1 (higher compliance effort), D 2.8 (depends on bureau/legal). Start **A + B**, then **E + F**.

**MVP.** A + B for Android mobile web (largest drop segment).

**KPIs.** Primary: submit rate (starts → submitted). Secondary: upload success rate, time to complete, approved→activated. Guardrails: fraud rate, manual-review backlog, complaints, approval quality (early delinquency indicators).

**Risks.** Fraud exploits easier flows; consent and data-protection for pre-fill; accessibility; vendor SDK reliability.

**Experiment plan.** A/B on Android web for 4 weeks; fraud and risk review weekly; compliance sign-off before launch.

**Example metrics (hypothetical).** Upload success 67% → 81%; submit rate 41.0% → 44.6%; fraud indicators unchanged; activation unchanged (E/F next).

**Recommendation.** Ship A + B; build status tracker and activation journey next quarter.

**Key PM lessons.** In regulated onboarding, partner with risk and compliance from day one; target the biggest technical failure point; measure through to activation, not just submission. *(Practical Project 1 develops this end to end.)*
