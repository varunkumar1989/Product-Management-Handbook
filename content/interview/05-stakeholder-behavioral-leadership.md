title: Stakeholder management (7 questions)
category: Stakeholder management
order: 11
summary: Alignment, conflict, influence and communication.
---

??? question "SH1 · Medium — How do you handle disagreement with an engineering lead?"
    **What it tests:** collaboration and respect for ownership.

    **Approach:** shared goal → their concern → evidence → options → test or decision rule → joint escalation if needed.

    **Model answer:** “Return to the shared goal; understand their concern (risk, cost, quality); bring evidence; explore options; agree a test or decision rule; if needed, escalate jointly with a written summary. I respect their ownership of *how* while I own the problem and priority.” *(Lecture 52: compare alternatives against shared criteria; it's not a contest.)*

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** time spent aligning vs speed.

    **Common mistakes:** overruling; or deferring on product decisions.

    **60-second answer:** goal → concern → evidence → test → document.

??? question "SH2 · Medium — Sales keeps promising features to customers. What do you do?"
    **What it tests:** process design and relationships.

    **Approach:** understand deals → give useful roadmap visibility → agree rules → create a request channel → close the loop.

    **Model answer:** “Understand the deals at stake; share roadmap themes rather than dates; agree that commitments need PM sign-off; give sales a fast way to log requests with customer evidence; close the loop on decisions so they trust the process.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** some strategic deals justify exceptions — decided explicitly.

    **Common mistakes:** blaming sales; no feedback loop.

    **60-second answer:** themes not dates + sign-off rule + request channel.

??? question "SH3 · Medium — How do you say ‘no’ to a senior leader?"
    **What it tests:** courage with tact.

    **Approach:** acknowledge goal → trade-offs with data → alternatives → their decision if it's theirs → document.

    **Model answer:** “Acknowledge the goal; show what we'd stop to do this, with data; offer alternatives (smaller version, later slot, quick test); if the decision is theirs, ask them to make it with the trade-off visible; document it.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** relationship vs focus.

    **Common mistakes:** a flat ‘no’; or a silent ‘yes’.

    **60-second answer:** “Yes, if we stop X — here are the options.”

??? question "SH4 · Hard — Two VPs want conflicting things from your team."
    **What it tests:** navigating politics with structure.

    **Approach:** clarify goals → map to company priorities → quantify options → recommendation → single approver → document.

    **Model answer:** “Clarify each VP's goal; map both to company priorities; quantify options; propose a recommendation; bring both to one decision meeting with a single approver (DACI); record the decision.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** the approver may be above both.

    **Common mistakes:** trying to satisfy both with half-measures.

    **60-second answer:** shared yardstick + one approver.

??? question "SH5 · Medium — How do you keep stakeholders informed without overwhelming them?"
    **What it tests:** communication design.

    **Approach:** map stakeholders → tier communications → no surprises.

    **Model answer:** “Stakeholder map; tiered communications — a weekly one-pager for leadership, channel updates for partners, release notes for the wider organisation; and early warnings on risks.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** frequency vs noise.

    **Common mistakes:** one giant status email for everyone.

    **60-second answer:** right message, right audience, right cadence.

??? question "SH6 · Medium — How do you work with designers?"
    **What it tests:** partnership mindset.

    **Approach:** problems and evidence → early involvement → shared metrics → specific feedback → tests.

    **Model answer:** “Bring problems and evidence, not solutions; involve designers in discovery; agree success metrics; give specific, user-centred feedback; protect time for edge states and accessibility; decide together what to test.” *(Module 5.)*

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** speed vs exploration time.

    **Common mistakes:** handing over wireframes as requirements.

    **60-second answer:** problem + evidence + shared metrics + tests.

??? question "SH7 · Medium — How do you handle a stakeholder who keeps changing requirements?"
    **What it tests:** scope management.

    **Approach:** understand why → change process → timeboxed discovery → sprint protection → transparent re-planning.

    **Model answer:** “Understand why (new information or an unclear goal); agree a change process with a decision log and impact assessment; timebox discovery to settle unknowns; protect the current sprint's scope; renegotiate dates transparently.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** some change is healthy learning.

    **Common mistakes:** absorbing every change silently.

    **60-second answer:** root cause + change process + visible impact.

id: behavioral-q
title: Behavioural (8 questions)
category: Behavioral
order: 12
summary: STAR stories — honest, specific, with real results or an honest "not measured".
---

!!! warning "Integrity rule"
    Use real stories. If you're transitioning into PM, use relevant experiences from your current role or course projects and label them accurately. Never invent metrics or titles (your course notes repeatedly flag this).

??? question "BE1 · Easy — Tell me about yourself."
    **What it tests:** clarity, relevance, motivation.

    **Approach:** present → past → future, ~90 seconds.

    **Model answer (template):** “I'm currently a [role] at [company], where I [product-relevant responsibility]. Before that, I [relevant experience]. I've been deepening PM skills through a 55-session product course and portfolio projects — for example, [project] where I [specific contribution]. I'm excited about this role because [specific reason tied to the company's product].”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** tailor to the role; depth vs brevity.

    **Common mistakes:** reciting your whole CV; generic motivation.

    **60-second answer:** the template, trimmed.

??? question "BE2 · Medium — Tell me about a time you influenced without authority."
    **What it tests:** influence, data use, collaboration.

    **Approach:** STAR with your specific actions.

    **Model answer (outline):** “S: the team wanted to build a large client's request; I believed checkout reliability mattered more. T: align sales, engineering and my manager. A: I quantified lost orders from funnel and support data, understood the client's underlying need, proposed a two-week workaround, and presented options with a recommendation. R: we fixed payment retries first; state the real measured change — or say it wasn't measured — and the client outcome.” *(Module 6.)*

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** be honest about what others did.

    **Common mistakes:** describing team actions as your own; invented numbers.

    **60-second answer:** S-T-A-R in four sentences.

??? question "BE3 · Medium — Tell me about a failure."
    **What it tests:** self-awareness and learning.

    **Approach:** real failure → your part → learning → what changed afterwards.

    **Model answer (structure):** “Describe a real failure you contributed to (e.g., shipping without a tracking plan, so you couldn't evaluate impact); own your part; what you learned; and evidence you changed behaviour (you now require a tracking plan in every PRD).”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** choose a meaningful but not catastrophic failure.

    **Common mistakes:** fake failures (“I work too hard”); blaming others.

    **60-second answer:** failure → my part → lesson → changed practice.

??? question "BE4 · Medium — Tell me about a time you used data to change a decision."
    **What it tests:** analytical influence.

    **Approach:** decision before → data and its limits → presentation → outcome → reflection.

    **Model answer (structure):** “The team planned X; I analysed Y (be specific about source and limits); I presented it as options with trade-offs; the decision changed to Z; the outcome was [real result or ‘not measured’]; next time I'd [improvement].”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** acknowledge uncertainty in the data.

    **Common mistakes:** presenting data as certainty.

    **60-second answer:** before → data → change → result.

??? question "BE5 · Medium — Describe a conflict with a teammate and how you resolved it."
    **What it tests:** maturity and empathy.

    **Approach:** situation → interests not positions → actions → outcome → relationship after.

    **Model answer (structure):** “Explain the disagreement neutrally; how you understood their interests; the options you explored; how you decided (test, data or escalation); the outcome and how the relationship improved.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** don't villainise the other person.

    **Common mistakes:** a story where you were simply right.

    **60-second answer:** interests → options → decision → relationship.

??? question "BE6 · Medium — A decision you made with incomplete information."
    **What it tests:** decision-making under uncertainty.

    **Approach:** known/unknown → cheap uncertainty reduction → reversible vs irreversible → decide → follow-up.

    **Model answer (structure):** “What you knew and didn't; how you reduced uncertainty cheaply (a few interviews, a quick data pull); whether the decision was reversible; what you decided and the checkpoint you set; what happened.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** speed vs certainty.

    **Common mistakes:** waiting for perfect data; or no follow-up.

    **60-second answer:** reduce uncertainty cheaply, decide, set a checkpoint.

??? question "BE7 · Medium — Why product management?"
    **What it tests:** genuine motivation and realistic expectations.

    **Approach:** pattern from your experience → what PM involves → why this company.

    **Model answer:** “Connect a real pattern from your work — noticing customer problems, coordinating teams, using data — to what PMs do; show you understand the hard parts (trade-offs, saying no, ambiguity); and tie it to this product.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** honesty about gaps you're closing.

    **Common mistakes:** “I want to be the CEO of the product.”

    **60-second answer:** pattern → understanding → fit.

??? question "BE8 · Hard — Tell me about the product you're most proud of."
    **What it tests:** ownership, depth and outcomes.

    **Approach:** problem and users → your role → key decisions and trade-offs → result → learning.

    **Model answer (structure):** “Problem and users; your specific role; two key decisions and their trade-offs; results (real numbers or honest qualitative evidence); what you learned. For career switchers, a portfolio project clearly labelled as such.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** confidentiality — share structure, not secrets.

    **Common mistakes:** feature list without decisions.

    **60-second answer:** problem → decisions → result → learning.

summary: Vision, teams, decisions, culture and senior-level judgement.
---

??? question "LE1 · Medium — How do you set a vision for your team?"
    **What it tests:** strategic leadership and inclusion.

    **Approach:** customer future → link to company strategy → co-create → translate → revisit.

    **Model answer:** “Describe a customer-centred future state, tied to company strategy, co-created with the team; translate it into strategy, outcomes and a roadmap; revisit it when evidence changes.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** inspiration vs concreteness.

    **Common mistakes:** a slogan with no strategy beneath it.

    **60-second answer:** customer future → strategy → outcomes.

??? question "LE2 · Hard — How do you build an outcome-focused culture in a feature-factory organisation?"
    **What it tests:** change leadership.

    **Approach:** start small → prove → change rituals → OKRs with guardrails → share learning → sponsor.

    **Model answer:** “Start with one team and one outcome, show results, change rituals (review outcomes, not outputs), introduce OKRs with guardrails, share learnings including failures, and enlist an executive sponsor.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** delivery commitments still exist; transition gradually.

    **Common mistakes:** mandating a framework top-down without proof.

    **60-second answer:** pilot → proof → rituals → scale.

??? question "LE3 · Medium — How do you develop junior PMs?"
    **What it tests:** coaching.

    **Approach:** expectations → ownership → feedback → pairing → scope growth.

    **Model answer:** “Set clear expectations; give ownership of a problem area; regular SBI feedback; pair on discovery and stakeholder meetings; review their writing; grow scope gradually.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** autonomy vs support.

    **Common mistakes:** doing their work for them.

    **60-second answer:** ownership + feedback + pairing.

??? question "LE4 · Hard — How do you make a high-stakes, irreversible decision?"
    **What it tests:** decision quality.

    **Approach:** classify reversibility → diverse input → scenarios → pre-mortem → criteria → decide → communicate → checkpoints.

    **Model answer:** “Separate reversible from irreversible decisions; gather diverse input; quantify scenarios; run a pre-mortem; set decision criteria in advance; decide; communicate the rationale; define checkpoints.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** speed vs certainty.

    **Common mistakes:** treating every decision as irreversible.

    **60-second answer:** reversibility → pre-mortem → criteria → decide.

??? question "LE5 · Medium — Your team is burnt out before a critical launch."
    **What it tests:** people leadership.

    **Approach:** protect people → trade-offs → honesty upward → learn.

    **Model answer:** “Cut scope, adjust the date if possible, remove distractions, rotate on-call, be honest with leadership about trade-offs, and run a planning retro afterwards.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** commitments to customers.

    **Common mistakes:** pushing harder.

    **60-second answer:** reduce load, communicate, learn.

??? question "LE6 · Hard — What would you do in your first 90 days as a PM here?"
    **What it tests:** onboarding plan and judgement.

    **Approach:** 30 learn → 60 focus → 90 deliver and plan.

    **Model answer:** “Days 1–30: learn users, data, team, strategy and stakeholders. Days 31–60: identify the top problem and a quick win; build relationships and rituals. Days 61–90: propose an outcome-based plan, deliver one improvement, share learnings.”

    **Assumptions & trade-offs:** urgent issues may compress learning.

    **Common mistakes:** big changes before understanding context.

    **60-second answer:** learn → focus → deliver.
